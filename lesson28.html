📚 Grammar — Countable & Uncountable Nouns

🔢 1. Countable Nouns

Countable nouns are things we can count.

one apple → two apples
one egg → three eggs
one banana → five bananas

We can use a/an with singular countable nouns.

a banana
an apple
an egg

🌾 2. Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns are things we normally do not count individually.

water
milk
rice
bread
cheese
meat

We normally don't say: two waters or three breads when talking about the substance itself.

➕ 3. Some

Use some mainly in affirmative sentences.

I have some apples.
There is some rice.
We need some water.

❓ 4. Any

Use any mainly in questions and negative sentences.

Do you have any milk?
I don't have any bread.

🔤 5. A / An

a + consonant sound
an + vowel sound
a banana
a tomato
an apple
an orange
an egg

🔢 6. Much / Many

many + countable plural nouns
much + uncountable nouns
How many apples do you have?
How many eggs are there?
How much water do you drink?
How much rice do you want?

💬 Real-life Conversation

Marta and Kevin are preparing breakfast.

Marta: Good morning, Kevin! Are you hungry?
Kevin: Good morning! Yes, I am very hungry.
Marta: What would you like for breakfast?
Kevin: I'd like some bread and an egg.
Marta: Do you want some fruit?
Kevin: Yes, please. Do we have any bananas?
Marta: Yes, there are three bananas.
Kevin: Great! Is there any milk?
Marta: Yes, there is some milk in the refrigerator.
Kevin: How much milk is there?
Marta: There is enough for breakfast.
Kevin: Perfect! Let's eat.

✍️ Writing Challenge

Write 6–8 sentences about your favorite food and drinks.

Try to use:

  • I like...
  • I don't like...
  • I eat...
  • I drink...
  • some
  • any
  • much / many

Example:

I like rice and chicken. I also like bananas. I usually eat an egg for breakfast. I drink some milk in the morning. I drink a lot of water every day. I don't drink much soda. I don't eat many sweets. My favorite drink is juice.
